Reading the numbers

  • Confidence: High is 70 percent or better, Moderate 60 to 70, Lean 55 to 60, Coin flip under 55. A High pick still loses about three times in ten.
  • Fair moneyline is what the simulated probability implies. Market is the nflverse line at build time; compare with current prices before acting.
  • "vs market" is the pick's model probability minus the market's vig-free probability. Positive means the model likes the pick more than the book does.
  • Injury and weather inputs come from web search summaries and, once published, the official injury report; refresh on game day.

What changed after Week 9

  • Week 9 went 14-1, the best week of the season for every method. Nine weeks pooled (135 games): blend 0.628, market moneyline 0.627, pure model 0.631. The model led the market in Weeks 1 to 5 and the market has led since Week 6.
  • Variance is back to 12.7 points. The steps to 13.5 and 14.0 chased two upset weeks; the 135-game grid favours 12 to 12.7, and the wider setting cost probability on favorites just as favorites started winning.
  • The market's share of the blend is now a flat 50 percent. The declining schedule assumed the model gains on the market as the season goes on; since Week 6 more market weight has been strictly better.
  • Quarterback changes count for more (weight 0.6 to 0.75), and the adjustment is now measured against the quarterback play already in the rating (2025 plus this season's actual starters) instead of 2025 alone.
  • The value table's cut rises from 2 to 5 percent expected value. Tuesday value bets are 30-53 for -$97 at flat $10; modest underdogs (+100 to +249) have won 10 of 38 against a modelled 43 percent. Treat the table as a list of disagreements with the market, not as proven edges.
